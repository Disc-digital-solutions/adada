# Validation kit — groundwork before any code

Three tools: the Facebook census, the supply questions, and the weekly numbers sheet.

---

## 1. The Facebook census

**Why.** The plan's numbers about this market are thin. Seven days of counting replaces them with facts,
decides L1's category list, and gives the first twenty sellers to invite.

**How.**
1. Pick the ten biggest groups where people in North Cyprus buy, sell and rent: Turkish-language
   groups, English-language expat groups, and student and nationality groups. Write each group's name,
   language and member count.
2. Once a day for seven days, at the same hour, scroll each group's posts from the last 24 hours and
   add tally marks to the sheet.
3. For twenty posts a day, come back after 48 hours and note whether the item is marked sold.
4. Note the twenty people or pages who post most.

Do not copy anyone's posts, photos or phone numbers. Count only.

- Which five kinds of post are most common? → L1's categories
- What share of posts are in Turkish, English, Arabic, other? → language order
- What share of followed-up items sold within 48 hours? → what "working" looks like
- How many posts have no price or no photo? → what the form must make easy
- How often does the same item get posted again? → the size of the "bump" problem
- Who are the twenty most active sellers? → the first people to invite

## 2. Supply questions

Ask in person or by voice note. Write the answer, not your impression of it.

### To the twenty active sellers

1. "May we put your items on our site for free? It takes us a minute."  **Yes / No**
2. Where do you post now, and how many times do you repost the same item?
3. What annoys you most about selling in the groups?
4. Would one link with all your items, for your Instagram bio, be useful to you?  **Yes / No**
5. Do buyers ever write to you in a language you do not read?

### To ten dorm operators

1. "May we list your dorm, with prices and photos, for free?"  **Yes / No**
2. How do students find you today? What share comes through agents?
3. What do you pay an agent per student?
4. Which months do you have empty beds?
5. What do you use to track rooms and payments — paper, Excel, software?
6. Would you answer requests that arrive by WhatsApp from a website?

### To twenty landlords or agencies

1. "May we list your flats for free?"  **Yes / No**
2. Do you rent to foreign students? If not, why not?
3. What would make you more willing — a checked ID, a university letter, an agency behind the student?
4. What do you ask for up front: deposit, commission, how many months' rent?
5. Which months are your flats empty?

### To any business (dealers, car rentals, tow trucks, cleaners, construction firms)

1. Where do your customers come from today?
2. **What do you pay for adverts each month, and to whom?**
3. Would a free page with your prices, photos and a WhatsApp button be useful?  **Yes / No**
4. What would you pay for being first in your category?

**The groundwork gate:** about 10 dorm operators and 20 landlords or sellers say yes in writing — a
WhatsApp message counts.

## 3. The weekly numbers sheet

Start it the week L1 launches. One row per week. The first column is the one that matters.

| Week of | **Listings with a tap in 7 days (%)** | New listings | Live listings | People who posted | Visitors | WhatsApp taps | Marked sold | Reports | Searches with no result (top 3) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | | | | | |

How to count the first column: of the listings posted 7 to 14 days ago, the share with at least one
WhatsApp or Call tap in their first 7 days. L1's gate is 30% (*proposed*).
